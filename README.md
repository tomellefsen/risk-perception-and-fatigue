Repository for the `Fear-Fatigue` model by Tom Ellefsen, Mariem Mami and Dorothée Perron, presented in the context of our integration project course *201-EYF-03 Projet en mathématiques*, H25, in the *Sciences de la nature* program at Cegep de l'Outaouais.

This repository contains several sub-directories:
1. **dash_app**: an interactive web dashboard for visualizing model behaviours and model parameter impact.
2. **data**: source and output data for our model fit.
3. **res**: data and report figures from model fit and simulations.
4. **src**: the source code for the infection model, the observation model, the analysis pipeline and the model configs.

A research report was written under the courses guidelines. A paper presenting our goal, hypothesis, methodology and results is underway.

**Abstract (old)**:

Epidemiology modeling has long been a crucial process that protects our societies and saves millions of lives by informing authorities when implementing public policies. Epidemiological models that take human behavior into account are new and emerging, but are still behind in considering phenomena that are becoming increasingly unavoidable. With this project, we propose an extension of the classic SIR model to demonstrate how the coupled relationship between perceived risk and behavioral fatigue influences the evolution of an epidemic using historical COVID-19 data from Quebec between 2020 and 2024. Our model successfully quantifies the deterioration of the public's behavioral response over the course of the pandemic and accurately describes historical dynamics, but only at the cost of significant compromises. It consistently describes not the reality in terms of the number of people infected, but the way our society reacts. Our work highlights the strengths and limitations of this approach to behavioral modeling, as well as the need to consider the impact of human behavior on the evolution of epidemics.
